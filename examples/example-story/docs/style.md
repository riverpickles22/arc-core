# The Keeper of Whitcombe Light — Prose Style Guide

> Governs the manuscript (`prose/**`). Directional docs say what the book is;
> this file says how its sentences behave. Canon YAML still wins on any
> question of fact (conventions §1).
>
> Like everything in this example story, it is deliberately small — enough to
> show the shape of a style contract (conventions §10), not enough to be a real
> book's voice. Copy `templates/style.md`, not this file.

## 1. The contract

- **POV.** Close third on Ines throughout. The dog is observed from outside —
  what Wren does and what Ines makes of it, never what Wren "thinks".
- **Tense.** Past.
- **The lamp is never a symbol.** The narrator describes tending it and never
  says what it stands for. If a sentence explains the light, cut it.
- **Withholding.** Why Ines came to Whitcombe is never stated outright.

## 2. Weather is work, not mood

The sea and the winter are the conditions of a job, not a commentary on how
anyone feels. Describe what they do to hands, glass, sleep, and supplies.

## 3. Rhythm

- Routine runs long: the stairs, the wick, the log, the walk down.
- Emergency runs short. The contrast is the alarm; do not also announce it.

## 4. Diction

- Nothing later than 1911, in object or in phrase.
- Concrete nouns from the working world — rope, oil, brass, salt.
- Comparisons stay inside the scene's world: weather, water, animals, tools.

## 5. Naming

- Ines names what a keeper would name. Charts and instruments get their proper
  names; the coastline gets hers.

## 7. Pre-draft checklist

Before showing any drafted scene to the author, verify:

1. Is the POV close third on Ines, with Wren observed from outside? (§1)
2. Does any sentence explain the lamp, or tell the reader what it means? (§1)
3. Does any word or object postdate 1911? (§4)
4. Does the reason Ines came to Whitcombe leak? (§1, §5)
5. Does sentence length track routine versus emergency? (§3)
6. Does the scene's frontmatter honestly list every event and fact the prose
   now rests on? (conventions §10)
